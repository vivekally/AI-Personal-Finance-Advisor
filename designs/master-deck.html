Arrive Finance
$8,000 of tax room disappears every December 31st you wait.

Part I · The Product · 10
Built responsibly
Trust isn't a feature. It's the architecture.
Software that holds opinions about $100,000 of someone's life savings has to be auditable, accountable, and honest about the limits of what it knows. Every one of these was a design constraint before it was a slide.
📚
“Education, not advice”
Every wizard output is labelled personalized financial education, which keeps us clear of Securities Act / OSC registration requirements for investment advice. Status: scoped and budgeted at $600–$1,000, counsel not yet engaged. This gates everything client-facing and is our first spend.
👤
Human in the loop, by design
AI drafts the brief → a licensed CFP reviews and annotates → the user executes. No autonomous trades, ever. Every recommendation carries a human signature before money moves. This is the safety rail robo-advisors removed and self-directed brokerages never had.
🇨🇦
PIPEDA + Canadian data residency
Supabase Postgres in ca-central-1, with row-level security. Aggregation runs through Flinks, 80% owned by National Bank of Canada — Big Six-backed infrastructure, not a screen scraper. Provider tokens AES-256 encrypted at the application layer before write.
📩
CASL-compliant messaging
Double opt-in on every channel. Timestamp and IP stored. STOP unsubscribes immediately and purges from the December reminder queue before it sends. Non-compliance runs to $10M per violation — the compliance work is cheaper than one mistake.
📊
Every claim carries a source
Primary sources throughout — IRCC, FCAC, CRA, Statistics Canada, FP Canada, CFP Board, Statista, DLA Piper. Estimates are labelled as estimates and our own multipliers are shown. Appendix G lists every one.
🚫
What we don't claim
Brokerage execution via SnapTrade is deferred to Year 2 pending IIROC/CSA review. Until then we surface guided execution — the user confirms inside their own broker. We are pre-revenue, pre-backend, and pre-counsel, and this deck says so on the slides where it matters.

RRSP year-1 = $0. TFSA from tax residency. FHSA room that does not exist until the account is opened. Student eligibility. T1135 thresholds on offshore savings. These are architectural assumptions, not features — a generalist app cannot retrofit them.
A deadline as the acquisition mechanic
Nobody markets a personal finance product on a date. A free FHSA deadline calculator is shareable in a way a subscription never is, and December 31 makes it seasonally viral, every year, forever. The wedge and the product are the same rules engine.
Ten languages, multi-script done right
Most products ship English, or English and French. We commit to the actual newcomer language mix and ship typography that respects Devanagari, Gurmukhi, right-to-left Arabic script and Simplified Chinese. Unglamorous, hard to retrofit, and precisely why it defends.
Human-in-the-loop AI for regulated guidance
Robo-advisors execute autonomously. Self-directed brokerages offer no guidance at all. Chatbots are confidently wrong exactly where error is most expensive. We sit in between: AI drafts, a licensed professional reviews, the user executes.
Part II · Market & Business · 13
Regulatory tailwind
Open banking is being built underneath us.
Royal Assent · March 26, 2026
Canada's Consumer-Driven Banking Act passed in March. Draft regulations landed in June. The framework is real and dated — but it is not switched on yet, and any deck that tells you otherwise hasn't read the Gazette.
✓
March 26, 2026 — Royal Assent
Bill C-15 replaced the 2024 Act with the full framework. The Bank of Canada took over as lead regulator from FCAC in late 2025.
●
June 27, 2026 — draft regulations, in consultation
Published in the Canada Gazette with a 60-day comment window. Phase 1 read access phases in after final publication — realistically 2027, not today.
○
Phase 2 — write access, payment initiation
Account switching and payment initiation. This is the phase that lets Arrive become a command layer rather than a read-only view.
○
Screen-scraping prohibition — legislated, not in force
The ban is written into the Act but has been deliberately held back pending further consultation. When it commences, every credential-sharing app must migrate to a regulated aggregator. We are already on one.

TD

The Big Six are legally required to expose customer data via API. No vendor risk and no scraping fragility once the framework commences.
What this actually means for us
We do not need the framework live to ship. Flinks is commercially available today and 80% owned by National Bank, so v1 aggregates real accounts on regulated infrastructure now. When the official APIs commence, connection quality improves and our compliance position strengthens — without a product rebuild. We are early to a dated, legislated shift. That is the rarest kind of timing: the risk is execution, not whether the wave arrives.

Appendix A · The advisor model
The same product, sold to the other side of the table.
B2B expansion path
The consumer product stays free and becomes purely an acquisition engine. Advisors pay — for software that lets them serve a segment they currently turn away, and for a pipeline of clients software alone never brings them. Everything in Part I is unchanged; only who pays changes.
Why an advisor buys this in 2026
The generic half of an advisor's value just became free, and they know it. What AI cannot give them is a qualified human relationship — so that is what we sell.

38%

The economics we fix
A newcomer with $50,000 invested earns an advisor roughly $500 a year — less than the cost of acquiring and onboarding them. So the advisor declines, and the highest-savings-rate cohort in the country goes unserved. We make an unprofitable client profitable by doing the acquisition, education and monitoring at near-zero marginal cost and handing over a pre-qualified file. The client grows; the advisor held the relationship from year one.
What lands on the advisor's desk
A client already through a seven-step intake in their own language, arriving as a file stating: TFSA room from residency start date rather than age · the FHSA deadline with a live countdown · RRSP room confirmed at $0 until a first return is filed · a T1135 flag where offshore savings trigger one · a ranked action list. The advisor reviews, adjusts and delivers it under their own licence. The system then flags clients with a deadline inside 90 days — a reason to call that is not a sales call.

Pricing hypothesis and the anchor beneath it
$200 per advisor per month ($2,400/year), plus a fee per qualified introduction. NaviPlan publishes $2,500 per licence per year, so the budget line already exists inside the practice — this is not a new category of spend. Pricing stays a hypothesis until the advisor calls below.
Who buys · who can veto
Buyer: an independent practice of one to ten licensed advisors, fee-based or hybrid, already paying for planning software. Two signals move a practice up the list — the advisor is themselves an immigrant or serves a diaspora community, and the practice wants more clients rather than more hours. Veto: the compliance officer, dealer or MGA, who cares where education ends and regulated advice begins. Designed for from the start.
The competitive set changes entirely
Not Thrive or Wealthsimple — Conquest Planning ($80M Series B, founded by NaviPlan's creator, claims 60%+ of Canada's advisor market), NaviPlan, RazorPlan, Snap Projections, Equisoft. All assume a client with full Canadian contribution history. None produces guidance in Hindi or Mandarin. None brings the advisor a client. Owning both surfaces is the position.
⚠ The honest risk — and the next test
Zero advisor conversations to date. The model rests on an untested buyer; one chat with a former RBC advisor is a start, not a finding. Next: 15 advisor calls from the FP Canada directory in high-immigration catchments — Brampton, Surrey, Mississauga, Scarborough, North York. Three questions: what did you spend acquiring your last five clients, how many newcomer prospects have you turned away, would you pay per qualified introduction.

Part III · Appendix B · 23
Appendix B · Community distribution
A newcomer non-profit already has the audience — and no way to answer this question.
Hypothesis · nothing approached or signed
Several national Canadian non-profits exist to welcome newcomers, and some run a free membership app — identity-verified at the moment of arrival, offering cultural and recreational benefits to hundreds of thousands of new permanent residents. They hold exactly the audience we need, at exactly the moment we need it. None has a financial product, and “what do I do with my savings?” is among the most common questions their members ask.
Why the fit is unusually good
🎯
Verified arrival date is our required input
Members are identity-verified and arrival-dated at signup — the single field the rules engine needs to be correct on the first screen. Every other channel makes us ask for it and hope.
🤲
Their mandate, our product
Financial settlement outcomes sit inside a newcomer-welcome mandate, but building a finance product is far outside their capability and their risk appetite. We give them a member benefit at zero build cost and zero liability — guidance labelled as education, with licensed escalation behind it.
📉
Near-zero CAC into a pre-qualified cohort
Against an estimated ~$40 community-channel CAC, an in-app benefit tile reaching an already-verified newcomer audience is the cheapest qualified acquisition open to us — and it compounds, because a new cohort arrives every year.
The same pattern, one tier down
Settlement agencies — ACCES Employment, COSTI and their equivalents in every major metro — run the same play at smaller scale, with faster decisions and lower governance overhead. They are already month 4–5 on the channel roadmap (slide 18). Start there to build the case study, then take it to the national organisation.
What we would actually propose
1
One benefit tile, co-branded
The free FHSA and TFSA deadline tool, placed as an in-app member benefit. No paywall inside the partnership.
2
A single-cohort pilot, 90 days
One arrival cohort, one metro. Measured on activation and on how many members discover room they did not know they had.
3
Explicit consent, no data sale
PIPEDA consent collected in their flow, in plain language. No member data leaves the partnership, and no bank referral runs inside it.
Why this could fail — and what we would do
Procurement moves in quarters, not weeks
A national non-profit decides slowly and by committee. This cannot be a channel the first 12 months depends on — which is why every channel above it on slide 18 runs without permission.
Anything resembling bank lead-gen kills it
If it reads as routing members to financial products for a fee, it dies in their governance — and deserves to. The bank referral model on slide 16 stays strictly outside this relationship.
Exclusivity would be a mistake for both sides
We should not ask and they should not grant it this early. A non-exclusive pilot with a clean exit beats a locked agreement we cannot yet service.
No organisation is named here by design — nothing has been approached, proposed or signed. A distribution hypothesis with a defined first test, at the same confidence level as Appendix A.
Part III · Appendix C · 24
Appendix C · United States
The same company, one rulebook over.
Year 3+ · nothing in this deck depends on it
Canada is the beachhead, not the prize. The United States has an immigrant population an order of magnitude larger and the identical problem under a different tax code — and critically, the core insight ports unchanged: eligibility for tax-advantaged accounts turns on tax status, not immigration status. In Canada that is residency for tax purposes; in the US it is resident-alien status under the substantial presence test. Same trap, same silence, same confidently wrong chatbot.
What ports directly
The intake wizard
Status, arrival year, income, goals, ranked plan. The shape of the interview is identical; only the account names change.
The multilingual layer
Spanish, Mandarin, Hindi, Tagalog, Vietnamese. The typography and locale architecture are already built for exactly this.
The deadline mechanic
US contribution deadlines are just as unknown and just as expensive to miss. A free deadline calculator is the same wedge.
Human-in-the-loop architecture
AI drafts, a licensed professional reviews, the user executes. The regulator changes; the safety design does not.
What has to be rebuilt
The rules engine itself
401(k), traditional and Roth IRA, HSA, 529 — not RRSP, TFSA, FHSA. The engine is a rewrite against the Internal Revenue Code, and that is a feature: it is the part competitors would also have to build.
The regulatory perimeter
SEC and FINRA rather than the CSA and provincial commissions, plus state-by-state variation with no federal single point. The “education, not advice” line has to be redrawn under US counsel, not translated.
Aggregation and distribution
No Consumer-Driven Banking Act. A different aggregator, a different bank-partnership landscape, and diaspora communities that cluster in different metros.

Part III · Appendix E · 26
Appendix E · Regulatory & compliance
How we stay on the right side of every regulator.
Risk → mitigation
“Education, not advice”
Open
All wizard output is labelled personalized financial education, keeping us clear of Securities Act and OSC registration requirements for investment advice. Counsel scoping call budgeted at $600–$1,000 and not yet engaged. This is the first gate and the first spend — it determines what we may say to a consumer with no advisor attached.
CASL — WhatsApp and email
Designed in
Double opt-in for all consent. Timestamp and IP stored at the point of consent. STOP unsubscribes immediately and purges from the December reminder queue before it sends. Penalties run to $10M per violation, so consent is built into the schema rather than bolted onto the send.
PIPEDA and data residency
Designed in
Supabase Postgres in ca-central-1 with row-level security. All aggregator tokens AES-256 encrypted at the application layer before write. No raw account data in plaintext logs. Aggregation runs through Flinks — regulated, and 80% owned by National Bank of Canada — not a screen scraper.
Brokerage execution
Deferred to Year 2
Order execution carries IIROC/CSA order-handling obligations we are not positioned to take on. Deferred. Until cleared, we surface guided execution only — the user reviews and confirms inside their own broker. We do not pretend to be a dealer.
Open banking — the actual timeline
✓
March 26, 2026 — Royal Assent
Bill C-15 repealed the 2024 Act and replaced it with the full Consumer-Driven Banking Act. The Bank of Canada leads implementation, having taken over from FCAC in late 2025.
●
June 27, 2026 — draft regulations published
Canada Gazette Part I, 60-day consultation. Rules phase in after final publication, which realistically places Phase 1 read access in 2027. It is not live today.
○
Screen-scraping prohibition — held back
Written into the Act but deliberately not brought into force, pending further consultation on timing and parameters. When it commences, credential-sharing apps must migrate to regulated aggregators.
○
Phase 2 — write access
Payment initiation and account switching. The phase that turns Arrive from a read-only view into a command layer.
Mandatory participants
RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC and National Bank are legally required to provide API access once the framework commences. No vendor risk and no scraping fragility in the end state.
⚠ What this means for our v1 plan
We do not need the framework live to ship, and we should not build a plan that assumes it. Flinks is commercially available today, so v1 aggregates real accounts on regulated Canadian infrastructure now. The official APIs are an upgrade to connection quality and compliance posture — not a dependency, and not a rebuild.

The advisor model, for comparison

Churn after the setup moment
The sharpest risk in the consumer model. A newcomer's acute need lasts weeks; the subscription needs to last months.
