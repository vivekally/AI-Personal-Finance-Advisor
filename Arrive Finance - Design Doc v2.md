## Problem Statement

Immigrants arriving in Canada save aggressively and invest poorly. They face a compounding trap: unfamiliar tax-advantaged accounts (RRSP, TFSA, FHSA), an unfamiliar market, no local financial network, and a constant fear of making an irreversible mistake.

**The majority path (80%):**
- Ask in newcomer Facebook/WhatsApp groups → get 10 conflicting answers → give up
- Wait 1-2 years, doing nothing, while contribution room expires silently
- Walk into a bank branch → walk out with a TFSA in a GIC at 3.5% → think they're solved

**The motivated path (20%, the Avdhesh profile):**
- Attempt Wealthsimple → overwhelmed by portfolio choice confusion → abandon
- Hire a human financial advisor ($200-400/hr, multiple calls) for account setup
- Return to the advisor for every major decision: RRSP deadline, home purchase, large investment

**The invisible mistake that defines year 1:** The FHSA. $8,000/year of tax room that expires on December 31st of the first eligible year. Most newcomers don't know it exists. Once the deadline passes, the room is gone. No one warns them.

## Target User & Sharpest Wedge

**Primary user:** Immigrants to Canada in years 1-3. Employed, $50K-$200K in savings, non-native English speaker (Hindi, Punjabi, Mandarin, Tagalog primary), unfamiliar with Canadian financial system, high anxiety about irreversible mistakes.

**The sharpest wedge user:** Year-1 newcomer approaching their first December 31st in Canada without knowing the FHSA deadline exists. $8,000 of tax room is about to expire. When they find out, they panic. That panic is the exact moment Arrive Finance needs to be in front of them.

**Canada admits 400,000+ permanent residents annually.** This cohort is concentrated (GTA, Vancouver, Calgary), identifiable (newcomer Facebook groups, Reddit r/PersonalFinanceCanada, settlement agencies), and highly motivated — they saved aggressively to immigrate and want those savings to work.

## Constraints

- **Regulatory:** Cannot be called "financial advice" without MFDA/IIROC registration. Framing: "personalized financial education and guidance." The specialist escalation rail (panel 3B) handles regulated advice through licensed CFPs.
- **Capital:** Pre-seed / self-funded. No burn runway for a 6-month build without revenue signal. The FHSA free tool (Approach C) can generate email list and signal before the platform ships.
- **Competition:** Thrive (closest competitor) is AI + RRSP/TFSA/FHSA, early access, not newcomer-specific. Newcomer-specific logic (RRSP year-1 = $0, FHSA Dec 31 deadline, multilingual trust signals) is the moat — hard to bolt on from a generalist app.
- **Timeline pressure:** Open banking Phase 1 is active (read access, early 2026). Build now; official bank APIs upgrade the aggregation layer in Phase 2 (mid-2027) without requiring a product redesign.
- **Fintech lawyer:** Engage before public launch. ~$600-1,000 for a 2-hour scoping call to confirm the "education not advice" framing holds.

## Key Premises (Agreed)

1. **Trust requires comprehensiveness.** An anxious newcomer with $80K in savings will not make financial decisions based on a single-feature tool. The product needs to show a complete picture — onboarding, health, and AI recommendations — for the target user to trust it enough to act. (Note: Approach B was challenged but rejected on this basis.)

2. **The FHSA deadline is the urgency hook.** Amber-badged, time-sensitive, in year-1 every time. This is the product's most defensible differentiator — a piece of knowledge that directly prevents a costly mistake most newcomers don't know they're about to make.

3. **The Facebook group user, not the Avdhesh user, is the majority.** The 80% who don't hire advisors are a larger market and a different product requirement: the tone must be approachable and reassuring, not sophisticated and technical. The design system (warm off-white, Fraunces headings, Plus Jakarta Sans body) is correct for this.

4. **Wealthsimple execution via SnapTrade (not Managed Investing).** Wealthsimple Managed Investing has no public API. SnapTrade connects to WS self-directed accounts and can guide users to buy XEQT/VGRO in their own account — one tap, no switching apps. This is the real execution story.

5. **Open banking is a tailwind, not a dependency.** Flinks (Phase 1, read access, now) handles aggregation today. Official bank APIs (Phase 2, mid-2027) upgrade the connection quality without requiring a product rebuild. Build on Flinks now.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full Platform Build ← CHOSEN
Build all 9 panels as working software (Onboarding wizard, Financial Health dashboard, AI Advisor), then launch.
- **Effort:** XL (4-6 months solo, 2-3 months with co-founder)
- **Risk:** High (no revenue signal for 4-6 months; Thrive moves in that window)
- **Pros:** Complete experience matches trust requirement; hardest to copy; integrated depth is a moat
- **Cons:** Long dark period; personal runway burn; might build wrong panels without early user data

### Approach B: Wizard-First
Build the RRSP/TFSA/FHSA wizard as MVP, show platform mockup as "coming soon," gate subscription at $20/month.
- **Effort:** M (3-5 weeks)
- **Risk:** Medium (revenue in 5 weeks but platform incompleteness may frustrate)

### Approach C: FHSA Tool → Wizard Sequence
Build a free FHSA deadline calculator as a viral hook (1 week), build email list, then launch wizard at $20/month.
- **Effort:** S (1 week for tool, then 4 weeks for wizard)
- **Risk:** Low-Medium (fastest signal but seasonal urgency)

---

## Recommended Approach

**Full Platform Build (Approach A)** — because the target user (anxious newcomer with $80K) will not trust a bare-minimum tool with their savings. The 9-panel experience, with the platform mockup as the design spec, is the right product to build.

**However:** Approach C (the FHSA free tool) should run in parallel as a marketing/email capture mechanism, not a product launch. It costs 1 week of effort and builds the list that the platform launch converts. Do not skip this.

**Recommended build sequence for Approach A:**

| Week | What Ships | Why |
|------|-----------|-----|
| 1 | FHSA deadline tool (free, email capture) | Email list before the platform builds. Dec 31 urgency is viral in newcomer communities. |
| 2-4 | Rules engine: RRSP/TFSA/FHSA calculator (TypeScript, Vitest tests) | The brain of the product. Must be correct — contribution room errors are the thing that destroys trust. |
| 5-6 | Auth (Clerk) + DB (Turso) + 7-step wizard UI | Wired to rules engine. Personalized plan output. |
| 7-8 | Stripe subscription ($20/month) + plan output screen | Paywall after the free plan preview. |
| 9-10 | Financial Health panels (All My Money via Flinks, Goals) | Aggregation layer. Real account data. |
| 11-12 | AI Advisor panels (Action Center, Arrive Score) | AI recommendations against goals. |
| 13+ | Advisor directory, SnapTrade execution layer | Human-in-the-loop and one-tap investment execution. |
